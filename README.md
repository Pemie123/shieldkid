# ShieldKit Local: the Easy Guide

**Block ads, trackers and scam websites for every app on your computer. Turn it on by typing one word: `shieldkid`.**

![How ShieldKit Local protects you: your apps connect through ShieldKit, which stops ads, trackers, scam sites and crypto-miners before they reach you](images/01-how-it-works.png)

> The pictures in this guide are *illustrations* of what you should see. Your screen may look a little different (colours, Windows version), but the words and steps are the same.

**Jump to:** [Windows](#windows) · [Mac](#mac) · [Linux](#linux) · [Is it working?](#step-4-check-that-it-is-on) · [Something not working?](#something-not-working)

---

## The 30-second version

**Windows**

1. Unzip the download.
2. Open the folder **windows-amd64**.
3. **Double-click `shieldkid.exe`**.
4. A blue window? Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
5. A black window opens and says **ShieldKit is ACTIVE**. Leave it open. You are protected.

**Mac or Linux:** follow [Step 3](#step-3-start-shieldkit). It takes four short lines.

---

## What you get

- ✅ **No more ads, trackers, scam and crypto-mining websites** (about 61,000 of them are blocked)
- ✅ **Warnings for fake bank and shop websites** (look-alikes of bpost, KBC, Belfius, PayPal and more)
- ✅ **Safer search results**: strict SafeSearch on Google, Bing and DuckDuckGo, and YouTube Restricted Mode
- ✅ **Pick a country** to browse from (you add the servers, see [Extra features](#extra-features-when-you-are-ready))
- ✅ **A star rating for any website**, combined from several review sites
- ✅ **A control panel** in your browser with counters and settings

Everything runs **on your own computer**. Nothing is sent to us.

## What you need

- A computer with **Windows 10/11, a Mac, or Linux**
- About **2 minutes**
- That's it. No account, no password, nothing else to install.

## Step 1: Unzip the download

| Your computer | How to unzip |
|---|---|
| **Windows** | Right-click `ShieldKitLocal.zip`, choose **Extract All…**, then click **Extract**. |
| **Mac** | Double-click `ShieldKitLocal.zip`. |
| **Linux** | Right-click → *Extract Here*, or type `unzip ShieldKitLocal.zip`. |

You now have a folder called **ShieldKitLocal**. Inside it are folders for each kind of computer.

## Step 2: Pick the right folder

![Six cards showing which folder to open: windows-amd64 for most Windows PCs, windows-arm64, macos-apple-silicon, macos-intel, linux-amd64, linux-arm64, with a tip for how to check](images/02-choose-your-file.png)

**Not sure?**

- **Windows:** open **Settings → System → About**. If *System type* says **x64-based processor**, use **windows-amd64**. If it says **ARM-based processor**, use **windows-arm64**.
- **Mac:** click the **Apple menu → About This Mac**. If you see **Chip: Apple M…**, use **macos-apple-silicon**. If you see **Processor: Intel**, use **macos-intel**.
- **Linux:** type `uname -m`. **x86_64** means **linux-amd64**. **aarch64** means **linux-arm64**.

Inside your folder is one program, called **shieldkid** (on Windows: **shieldkid.exe**).

## Step 3: Start ShieldKit

<a id="windows"></a>

### Windows: the quickest way (just double-click)

![Four steps: double-click shieldkid.exe; if a blue window appears click More info; click Run anyway; a black window opens saying ShieldKit is ACTIVE](images/03-windows-double-click.png)

1. Open the **windows-amd64** folder (or **windows-arm64**).
2. **Double-click `shieldkid.exe`.**
3. If a **blue window** says *"Windows protected your PC"*, click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
   *Why this appears: the program is not "signed" with a paid certificate. That is normal for free programs.*
4. A **black window** opens and says **ShieldKit is ACTIVE**. 🎉

> **Keep the black window open** while you use your computer (you can minimise it).
> To turn ShieldKit off, click in the black window and press **Ctrl+C**. (Closing the window works too.) Your settings go back to normal.

**Next time:** double-click it again. Or do the next part once, and you can simply type `shieldkid`.

### Windows: install it (so you can just type `shieldkid`)

This is optional. It saves you from finding the folder every time.

**A. Open a black window in the right folder.** (Do this once.)

![Three steps: click the address bar of the folder, type cmd, press Enter. A black window opens](images/04-windows-open-terminal.png)

1. In the folder window, **click once** in the long white box at the top (the address bar).
2. Type **`cmd`** and press **Enter**.
3. A **black window** opens. You are now in the right place.

> 💡 **Windows 11 shortcut:** right-click an empty spot inside the folder and choose **Open in Terminal**. This gives you a blue window (called PowerShell).
> 💡 **In a blue PowerShell window** everything works the same, but you must put `.\` in front: `.\shieldkid.exe install`.

**B. Type the install command.**

![A black Command Prompt window showing the command shieldkid.exe install and the lines Installed to and Added to your user PATH](images/05-windows-install.png)

Type this and press **Enter**:

```
shieldkid.exe install
```

You should see **Installed to …** and **Added … to your user PATH**. No password is needed.

**C. Close that window and open a brand-new one.** (Press the Windows key, type `cmd`, press Enter.)

Now type:

```
shieldkid
```

and press Enter. That's it! From now on, `shieldkid` works from any folder.

<a id="mac"></a>

### Mac

![A Terminal window showing four commands: cd to the folder, chmod +x shieldkid, xattr -d com.apple.quarantine shieldkid, and ./shieldkid install](images/06-mac-install.png)

1. Open the **Terminal** app. (Press **Cmd + Space**, type `Terminal`, press **Enter**.)
2. Type `cd ` (with a space after it), then **drag your folder** from Finder onto the Terminal window and press **Enter**. For an Apple-chip Mac that folder is **macos-apple-silicon**.
3. Type these three lines, pressing **Enter** after each one:

```
chmod +x shieldkid
xattr -d com.apple.quarantine shieldkid
./shieldkid install
```

What they do: the first lets the file run as a program, the second tells your Mac you trust it (it is not signed by Apple), the third installs it. No password is needed.

4. **Close Terminal and open a new Terminal window.** Type `shieldkid` and press **Enter**.

> If a Mac asks for permission to change network settings, allow it. If it refuses, ShieldKit says so and you can still use it. See [Something not working?](#something-not-working).

<a id="linux"></a>

### Linux

Open a terminal in your folder (for most computers: **linux-amd64**) and type:

```
chmod +x shieldkid
./shieldkid install
```

Then **open a new terminal window** and type `shieldkid`.

> On **GNOME desktops** (Ubuntu, Fedora and most others) ShieldKit also switches on your system proxy automatically. On other desktops it says **NOT protected yet**. Use `shieldkid run` + a program, or the proxy address `127.0.0.1` port `8899` (see [Something not working?](#something-not-working)).

## Step 4: Check that it is on

![A terminal showing ShieldKit is ACTIVE, the proxy address, the control panel address and the note that system proxy settings now point at ShieldKit](images/07-type-shieldkid.png)

When you type `shieldkid` you should see **ShieldKit is ACTIVE**.

- **Leave that window open.** ShieldKit works while it is open.
- The **Apps** line tells you whether your apps are protected automatically.
  If it says **NOT protected yet**, it tells you why. See [Something not working?](#something-not-working).

### Quick tests (optional)

**Is it on?** Open a *second* terminal window and type:

```
shieldkid status
```

You should see **ShieldKit is ACTIVE** and a count of what it has blocked.

**Does it really block ads?** This sends a pretend ad request through ShieldKit. If it prints **204**, ShieldKit stopped it:

| Windows | Mac / Linux |
|---|---|
| `shieldkid run curl.exe -s -o NUL -w "%{http_code}" http://doubleclick.net/` | `shieldkid run curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}" http://doubleclick.net/` |

**See what it has done:** type `shieldkid panel` to open the control panel.

## The control panel

![The control panel: a dark header with an on/off switch, tabs named Overview, Protection, Location, Check a site and Use in apps, counters of what was blocked, and a list of recent activity with Allow buttons](images/08-control-panel.png)

Type `shieldkid panel` and your browser opens it. (The address is **http://127.0.0.1:8899/** unless you chose another port.)

- **Overview:** counters, and a list of what was just stopped. If a site you trust gets blocked by mistake, press **Allow**.
- **Protection:** switch each kind of protection on or off.
- **Location:** pick a country to browse from.
- **Check a site:** type a website and get one combined star rating.
- **Use in apps:** copy-and-paste settings for apps that need them.
- The switch at the top right turns **all** filtering on or off.

## Something not working?

![Six cards: It says NOT protected yet; shieldkid is not recognized; a website stopped working; my internet feels broken after a crash; address already in use; Windows or Mac says unknown or unrecognised. Each card says what to do](images/10-something-not-working.png)

### "It says NOT protected yet"
Your computer did not allow ShieldKit to switch on the automatic setup. Nothing is broken, and you have two easy options:

- **For one program:** `shieldkid run` + the program, for example `shieldkid run git pull`.
- **For an app with its own proxy setting:** enter **`127.0.0.1`** as the address and **`8899`** as the port. The control panel's **Use in apps** tab has copy-and-paste lines for Windows, Mac and Firefox.

### "'shieldkid' is not recognized" (Windows) or "command not found" (Mac/Linux)
The terminal does not know about it yet.

1. **Close the window and open a new one** after you ran `install`.
2. Still not working? On Windows, just **double-click `shieldkid.exe`**. On Mac or Linux, open the folder in Terminal and type `./shieldkid`.

### A website stopped working
ShieldKit may have blocked something that site needs.

1. Type `shieldkid panel`.
2. Open **Overview** and find the site under **Recent activity**.
3. Press **Allow**.

### My internet feels broken after a crash or power cut
Type:

```
shieldkid off
```

That puts your network settings back. (Starting `shieldkid` again does the same thing.) Even if you do nothing, apps fall back to a normal connection when ShieldKit is not running, so you should not lose your internet.

### "address already in use"
ShieldKit is probably already running. Type `shieldkid status` to check. To use a different port: `shieldkid --listen 127.0.0.1:8898`.

### Windows or Mac says the program is "unknown" or "unrecognised"
This is normal for free programs that are not signed.

- **Windows:** click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **Mac:** run the `xattr -d com.apple.quarantine shieldkid` line from Step 3. (Or open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and press **Open Anyway**.)

### YouTube video ads or ads inside web pages are still there
ShieldKit Local blocks ad *websites*, not ads built into a page or a video. For those, use the **ShieldKit browser extension** next to it.

### A few more questions

- **Do I have to keep the window open?** Yes. Closing it turns ShieldKit off. You can minimise it.
- **Does it work for every app?** For most. Apps that ignore your system settings need `shieldkid run` or the proxy address (`127.0.0.1`, port `8899`).

## Words explained

| Word | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **Terminal** (or *Command Prompt*) | A window where you type a command instead of clicking. On Windows it is black. |
| **Folder** | The same thing as a directory. A box that holds files. |
| **Proxy** | A "go-between". Your apps send their requests to ShieldKit first, and ShieldKit decides what is allowed through. |
| **Control panel** | The settings page ShieldKit shows in your browser. |
| **Unsigned** | The program does not have a paid "publisher" certificate. Windows and Mac warn about this for free software. |

## Extra features (when you are ready)

**Protect just one program.** `shieldkid run git pull` runs that one command with ShieldKit in front of it, without changing any setting on your computer.

**Browse from another country.** In the control panel open **Location**. Every country is listed. You add the servers: your own, or ones you are allowed to use. ShieldKit checks that your address really changed, and if the server stops working it blocks traffic instead of showing your real address.
*Free public servers belong to strangers and can see which sites you visit. Never sign in to banking or email through them.*

**Rate a website.** Type `shieldkid check bol.com` and you get one star rating, combined from Trustpilot, Sitejabber and Reviews.io (and Google if you add your own key).

**What it cannot do.** ShieldKit does not open your *encrypted* web traffic, because that would be a security risk. So it cannot hide ads that are part of a page, or remove video ads on YouTube. The browser extension does that.

## Is it safe?

- It **only listens on your own computer**. Other computers on your network cannot reach it.
- Your **passwords and keys never leave** the program.
- A website you visit **cannot control** ShieldKit.
- Your original network settings are **saved before** anything changes, and put back when it stops.

## Uninstall

1. Press **Ctrl+C** in the ShieldKit window.
2. Type `shieldkid uninstall`. (This removes the installed program and lets the terminal forget it. Your settings folder is left alone.)
3. Delete the **ShieldKitLocal** folder you unzipped.
